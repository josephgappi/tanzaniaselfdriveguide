Tanzania · Self-Drive Updates

What is changing on the ground?

Wildlife movements, road conditions, park rules, access information and other practical changes can affect a self-drive safari. This page brings the most useful updates together in one place.

Always confirm time-sensitive rules and access information with the relevant authority before departure.
Latest Update 7 October 2026
Wildlife · Central & Northern Serengeti

Wildebeest are on the move — plan your route around the migration.

October is a transition period in the Serengeti–Mara ecosystem. Herds can be spread across the northern ecosystem and begin moving south as rainfall and fresh grazing change. Exact locations can change quickly, so self-drivers should treat migration maps as planning guidance rather than a guarantee.

What this means for self-drivers
  • Build flexibility into your Serengeti route.
  • Allow extra time for wildlife stops and slower park-road driving.
  • Check current conditions before committing to a remote section.

Why this page exists

Updates that actually matter when you are driving yourself.

A self-drive trip can be affected by things that are not obvious when you first build your itinerary: a changing wildlife pattern, a rough road after rain, a park access requirement, a tariff change or a route that simply takes longer than expected.

The purpose of this page is not to publish every piece of safari news. It is to highlight information that can change the way a self-driver plans, drives or chooses a route.

For important rules, fees and access arrangements, use the linked official authority sources as the final reference before travel.

Self-driver rule

If an update changes access, fees, opening arrangements or park rules, check the official authority directly before relying on it.

Wildlife updates are different: they are naturally fluid, so use them to shape your route rather than expecting an exact guarantee.

Before you drive

A five-minute update check can save a long detour.

Use this checklist on the day before entering a remote area.

1

Check the park authority

Look for current visitor information, tariffs, rules and official notices.

2

Check the road

Ask about rain, washouts, corrugation, construction and difficult sections.

3

Check fuel

Do not enter a long remote section assuming the next fuel stop will be convenient.

4

Check your route

Confirm the gate, camp, road and destination are compatible with your day's plan.

5

Check the vehicle

Tyres, spare wheel, recovery equipment, fuel and communication matter more in remote areas.

6

Check the weather

Rain can turn a straightforward track into a much slower or more difficult journey.
