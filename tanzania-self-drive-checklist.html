Tanzania Self-Drive Safari Checklist

Everything to check before setting off on an independent safari in Tanzania — from documents and vehicle inspection to fuel, food, navigation and camping.

Use this as your final preparation checklist before leaving your starting point.

Prepare Before You Leave

A successful self-drive safari is not just about choosing a route and renting a 4x4. Before you leave, there are several practical things that are easy to forget.

Your documents need to be in order, your vehicle should be properly inspected, your navigation should work offline and you should know where your next fuel and resupply opportunities are.

This checklist is designed to help you work through those details before you start driving.

Quick Answer: What Should I Prepare?

At minimum, prepare your travel documents, driving documents, rental paperwork, insurance information, navigation, communication, money, fuel plan, food and water.

If you are camping, add your complete camping equipment and food plan. If you are driving a rental 4x4, inspect and photograph the vehicle before leaving.

Master Pre-Trip Checklist

Work through this list before your first major driving day.

1. Travel Documents

Make sure your essential personal documents are ready.

Passport Check that it is valid for your planned travel period.
Visa / entry requirements Check the current requirements that apply to your nationality before travelling.
Driving licence Carry your valid home-country driving licence and confirm what additional documentation is required for your circumstances.
Copies of important documents Keep digital copies and, where practical, paper copies of important documents.

2. Rental & Vehicle Documents

Know exactly what you are agreeing to before driving away.

Rental agreement Read the important terms, permitted routes, return conditions and additional charges.
Insurance information Understand what is covered and what is excluded.
Security deposit / bond information Confirm the amount, conditions for deductions and the return process.
Emergency and roadside assistance contacts Save the rental company's emergency number before leaving.
Vehicle return instructions Know where, when and how the vehicle must be returned.

3. Inspect the 4x4 Before Leaving

Do not rush through vehicle handover. The inspection you complete before leaving can be important later if there is a disagreement about damage.

Exterior

  • Body panels
  • Bumpers
  • Mirrors
  • Windows
  • Windshield
  • Lights
  • Roof and roof equipment
  • Existing scratches and dents

Tyres & Wheels

  • All four main tyres
  • Spare tyres
  • Tyre condition
  • Wheel condition
  • Jack
  • Wheel spanner
  • Tyre-pressure equipment if supplied

Recovery Equipment

  • Recovery straps
  • Shackles / recovery points
  • Shovel if supplied
  • Compressor if supplied
  • Other recovery tools
  • Know where everything is stored

Vehicle Systems

  • Engine starts normally
  • Lights work
  • Indicators work
  • Wipers work
  • Air conditioning works
  • Fridge works if included
  • Power outlets / inverter if included

Photograph the Vehicle

Take clear photographs and short videos of the vehicle before departure. Photograph all sides, the roof, wheels, windscreen, windows and any existing scratches, dents or other damage.

If something is already damaged, make sure it is recorded according to the rental company's inspection procedure before you leave.

4. Understand Your Insurance

Do not assume that “comprehensive insurance” means every part of a safari vehicle is automatically covered.

Ask What Is Covered

  • Collision damage
  • Theft
  • Third-party liability
  • Glass
  • Tyres
  • Underbody damage
  • Recovery costs

Ask What You Pay

  • Excess / deductible
  • Security deposit
  • Tyre damage
  • Glass damage
  • Underbody damage
  • Recovery or towing
  • Driver-caused damage
Important:

Insurance conditions differ between rental companies. Read the actual rental agreement rather than relying only on a description on a website.

5. Navigation & Communication

Mobile coverage is not guaranteed everywhere. A self-drive traveler should prepare to navigate even when the phone has no useful signal.

Download offline maps Make sure the areas you plan to drive through are available offline.
Carry a second navigation option Do not rely on a single phone or application for remote travel.
Charge your devices Start each driving day with your phone and navigation devices charged.
Carry charging cables Bring the cables and adapters needed for your phones and other equipment.
Save emergency contacts Store your rental company, accommodation and relevant emergency contacts.

6. Money & Payments

Do not assume that every payment point on your route will work exactly like the ones you are familiar with at home.

Before Leaving

  • Carry a suitable amount of local currency
  • Have a working payment card
  • Keep a backup payment method
  • Separate emergency cash from daily spending money

Keep It Secure

  • Do not display large amounts of cash unnecessarily
  • Use secure storage in the vehicle where appropriate
  • Keep cards and documents protected
  • Carry emergency funds separately

7. Fuel Planning

Fuel planning is one of the most important parts of independent driving.

Start with a full tank where practical Begin longer remote sections with sufficient fuel.
Know your next fuel opportunity Do not assume there will be a station exactly where you expect one.
Monitor the fuel gauge Refuel before reaching a critically low level.
Ask about the vehicle's range Know the approximate range of your specific vehicle under safari conditions.

8. Food, Water & Groceries

If you are camping, grocery planning becomes part of your route planning.

Food

  • Breakfast supplies
  • Lunch supplies
  • Snacks
  • Tea / coffee
  • Cooking ingredients
  • Basic seasonings
  • Food for unexpected delays

Water

  • Drinking water
  • Cooking water
  • Extra reserve for remote sections
  • Suitable water storage containers
Plan your resupply:

Arusha is a useful place to complete major grocery shopping before leaving for the northern circuit. Depending on your route, Karatu and Mto wa Mbu can also be useful places to resupply.

9. Camping Equipment Checklist

If your trip includes camping, confirm exactly what your rental company provides rather than assuming that camping equipment is included.

Sleeping

  • Roof tent or ground tent
  • Mattress
  • Sleeping bag / bedding
  • Pillows
  • Blankets if required

Cooking

  • Camping stove
  • Fuel for stove
  • Pots and pans
  • Plates and cups
  • Cutlery
  • Cooking utensils

Camp

  • Table
  • Chairs
  • Lighting
  • Cooler / fridge if included
  • Rubbish bags

Personal

  • Headlamp
  • Warm layer
  • Rain protection
  • Personal toiletries
  • Quick-dry clothing

10. What to Pack Personally

Lightweight clothing Choose practical clothing suitable for warm daytime conditions.
Warm layer Some mornings and evenings can be cool, especially at higher elevations.
Rain protection Pack suitable rain protection according to your travel season.
Comfortable closed shoes Useful for walking around campsites and during travel days.
Sun protection Hat, sunglasses and suitable sunscreen.
Personal medication and essentials Carry anything you personally need for the duration of the journey.

11. Safety & Emergency Preparation

Self-drive means you have more responsibility for your own preparation. The goal is not to expect problems, but to be prepared if something unexpected happens.

Before Departure

  • Save roadside assistance contacts
  • Save accommodation contacts
  • Tell someone your planned route
  • Check the day's weather where relevant
  • Know your next overnight location

Vehicle Problem

  • Move to a safe position where possible
  • Contact your rental company
  • Use recovery equipment only when appropriate
  • Do not take unnecessary risks
  • Follow local park and road rules

Do Not Rely Only on Your Phone

A phone can lose signal, run out of battery or become damaged. Keep important information available offline and know how to contact your rental company without depending on a single device.

12. Before Entering a National Park

Confirm park access requirements Check the current rules and procedures for the park you are entering.
Confirm permits and fees Know what arrangements are required for your visit.
Check opening and closing times Plan your arrival and departure accordingly.
Carry enough fuel and water Remote park driving requires appropriate preparation.
Know the park rules Follow speed limits, wildlife-distance rules, camping rules and other current regulations.
Always verify current regulations:

Park procedures, fees and access requirements can change. Check official information before your trip.

Your Final Morning Checklist

Before you drive away from your starting point, take five minutes to go through this final list.

  1. Passport and important documents are secure.
  2. Driving licence and required driving documents are with you.
  3. Rental agreement and insurance information are accessible.
  4. Vehicle damage has been photographed and recorded.
  5. Tyres and spare tyres have been checked.
  6. Recovery equipment is present and accessible.
  7. Fuel level is sufficient for the first section of the journey.
  8. Drinking water is loaded.
  9. Food and camping supplies are loaded if required.
  10. Offline maps are downloaded.
  11. Phones and power banks are charged.
  12. Emergency contacts are saved.
  13. Your first overnight location is known.
  14. You understand the day's route and approximate driving conditions.

Frequently Asked Questions

What documents do I need for a self-drive safari in Tanzania?

Carry your passport, required entry documents, valid driving licence and any documents required by your vehicle rental company. Check current entry and driving requirements before travelling.

Do I need an international driving permit to drive in Tanzania?

Requirements can depend on your nationality, licence and rental arrangement. Confirm current requirements with the relevant authorities and your rental company before travelling.

What should I check when collecting a rental 4x4 in Tanzania?

Check the tyres, spare tyres, recovery equipment, lights, fluids, bodywork, windows, communication equipment and camping equipment if included. Photograph existing damage and make sure the rental agreement records it.

Should I buy food before starting a Tanzania self-drive safari?

If you are camping, buying essential food and water before leaving your main resupply point is useful. Plan additional resupply stops according to your route.

Do I need offline maps for a Tanzania self-drive safari?

Offline navigation is strongly recommended because mobile coverage can vary, particularly in remote areas. Carry more than one navigation option where practical.

What should I do before leaving Arusha with a rental 4x4?

Complete the vehicle inspection, photograph the vehicle, check tyres and recovery equipment, confirm insurance and rental conditions, obtain emergency contacts, buy essential supplies and make sure your navigation works offline.
