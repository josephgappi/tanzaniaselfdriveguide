Start with the basics

What is self-drive camping in Tanzania actually like?

A Tanzania self-drive camping safari gives you the freedom to travel independently, carry your own camping equipment and stay at designated campsites along your route.


The experience is very different from staying in lodges every night. You need to think about where you will sleep, where you will buy food, how much water to carry, cooking equipment, campsite rules, wildlife safety and how far you are travelling between towns.


Good preparation makes the difference. You do not need to carry everything from the first day, but you should understand where your next reliable resupply opportunities are before entering remote safari areas.

Quick answer

Yes, camping is practical for a Tanzania self-drive safari. The most important things are choosing an appropriate 4x4, using authorized campsites, carrying enough food and water, understanding wildlife safety and planning your route around fuel and resupply opportunities.

Camping options

What types of camping can you use?

The right camping setup depends on your vehicle, route, comfort level and how much equipment you want to carry.

Roof Tent Camping

A roof tent is one of the most popular setups for self-drive safari travel. The sleeping area is raised above the ground and attached to the vehicle.

  • Raised sleeping platform
  • Quick campsite setup
  • Works well with safari 4x4s
  • Keeps your sleeping area off the ground

Ground Tent Camping

Ground tents are another practical option. They can provide more flexibility in sleeping arrangements and may be suitable for travelers who prefer a conventional tent.

  • Flexible sleeping arrangements
  • Easy to move between campsites
  • Suitable for different group sizes
  • Requires careful wildlife safety procedures

Fully Equipped Safari Vehicles

Some Tanzania self-drive rental vehicles are equipped specifically for camping, including tents, mattresses, cooking equipment, recovery equipment and storage.

  • Camping equipment carried in the vehicle
  • Cooking equipment
  • Recovery equipment
  • Designed for safari roads
Campsites

Where can you camp in Tanzania?

Camping arrangements vary between national parks, conservation areas and other parts of Tanzania. Always check the current rules for the specific area on your route.

Public Campsites

Public campsites generally provide basic facilities shared by multiple travelers. They can be a practical option for independent safari travelers who want a simple camping experience.

  • Basic camping facilities
  • Shared facilities
  • Suitable for independent travelers

Special Campsites

Special campsites can offer a more private camping experience. Availability and booking requirements depend on the relevant authority and location.

  • More private experience
  • May require advance arrangements
  • Check current regulations

Camps Outside Parks

There are also campsites, lodges and private camping facilities outside protected areas. These can be useful for overnight stops before or after entering a national park.

  • Useful for route planning
  • Can provide additional services
  • Often close to towns

Food & Water

Food and water planning for self-drive camping

Food and water planning is one of the biggest differences between a lodge safari and a self-drive camping safari. You need to think ahead about how many days you will be away from reliable shopping opportunities.

Food planning

Keep meals simple enough to prepare at camp, especially on days when you have spent many hours driving.

  • Breakfast supplies
  • Bread and cereals
  • Rice and pasta
  • Vegetables and fruit
  • Easy lunch ingredients
  • Snacks
  • Tea and coffee
  • Cooking oil, salt and spices

Water planning

Water is more important than simply calculating how much you expect to drink. Allow for cooking, personal use, delays and unexpected changes to your route.

  • Drinking water
  • Cooking water
  • Emergency reserve
  • Secure containers
  • Knowledge of your next resupply point
Where to shop

Where can I buy groceries for a Tanzania self-drive camping safari?

Arusha is the easiest place to do your main grocery shopping before starting a northern Tanzania self-drive camping safari. You can also use Karatu and Mto wa Mbu as practical resupply stops along the northern circuit.

Arusha: do your main shopping here

Arusha has large supermarkets, local markets and smaller shops, so you have more choice before leaving town for the safari areas.

For a longer camping trip, it is sensible to buy your important long-lasting supplies in Arusha: drinking water, breakfast food, cooking ingredients, snacks and other items that you may not easily find later on the route.

Fresh fruit and vegetables can also be purchased before departure.

Supermarkets in Arusha

Two supermarket options you can check before your trip are Village Supermarket and Shoppers Supermarket.

Village Supermarket — AIM Mall
Village Supermarket officially lists its Arusha location at AIM Mall, Dodoma Road, Majengo, Arusha. It also has another Arusha location in the Themi/Njiro area.

Local markets

Local markets can be useful for fresh fruit, vegetables and other everyday food supplies.

For campers, buying fresh produce shortly before leaving Arusha can be useful because you do not have to carry it for the entire trip.

If you are unfamiliar with local shopping, ask your accommodation, vehicle rental company or local contact for current advice on where to shop along your route.

Karatu: a useful resupply stop

Karatu is a practical place to consider topping up basic food supplies before continuing towards the Ngorongoro area and Serengeti.

You can use local shops and food markets for suitable everyday supplies and fresh produce. However, availability can vary, so it is better to treat Karatu as a top-up opportunity rather than your only shopping plan.

Mto wa Mbu: another top-up option

Mto wa Mbu is another useful stop on the northern safari route. It is particularly convenient for travelers passing through the Lake Manyara area before continuing towards Ngorongoro and Serengeti.

Basic food supplies and fresh produce can be purchased locally, but travelers should still leave Arusha with the important supplies they know they will need.

What should you buy?

  • Drinking water
  • Bread and breakfast foods
  • Rice and pasta
  • Fresh fruit and vegetables
  • Cooking oil
  • Tea and coffee
  • Snacks
  • Salt and spices
  • Easy-to-cook meals
  • Any special dietary products

A simple grocery strategy

1. Shop properly in Arusha. Buy the main supplies you will need before entering the more remote safari areas.

2. Add fresh produce. Buy fruit and vegetables according to the number of days you expect to spend away from town.

3. Top up in Karatu or Mto wa Mbu. If your route passes through these towns, use them as additional opportunities to replenish suitable supplies.

4. Do not depend on park shopping. National parks should not be treated like towns with supermarkets. Enter remote areas prepared.

Wildlife safety

Camping safely around wildlife

Camping in safari areas is different from camping in an ordinary campground. Wildlife can move through areas around campsites, so campsite discipline matters.

Never assume wildlife is far away

Animals can move near campsites, especially during the night. Follow campsite instructions and never approach wildlife.

Keep food secured

Do not leave food scattered around the campsite. Store food and rubbish appropriately and follow the rules of the campsite.

Be careful after dark

Use a torch when moving around camp after dark and avoid unnecessary movement away from your tent or vehicle.

Listen to campsite staff

Local campsite staff and park authorities understand the conditions in their area. Follow their instructions even if you have previous camping experience.

Seasons

When is camping comfortable in Tanzania?

Camping conditions change through the year. Temperature, rainfall and road conditions can all affect your experience.

Dry periods

Dry conditions can make many safari roads easier to travel and generally make camping more predictable. Nights can still be cool in some areas.

Rainy periods

Rain can make some roads slower and more challenging. A suitable 4x4, careful driving and flexible planning become particularly important.

Cool nights

Higher elevations, including the Ngorongoro area, can feel significantly cooler at night than daytime safari temperatures.

Important route detail

Camping around Ngorongoro

Ngorongoro is managed separately from Tanzania's national park system, so travelers should check the current conservation-area rules and procedures before travelling.

Check current NCAA requirements before your trip

Rules, fees, camping arrangements and access procedures can change. Always check the current information from the Ngorongoro Conservation Area Authority before your journey.

Avoid these mistakes

Common Tanzania self-drive camping mistakes

Most problems can be reduced by planning the route, equipment and resupply points before leaving town.

Trying to carry too little food

Remote safari areas do not offer the same shopping options as Arusha.

Not carrying enough water

Plan for drinking, cooking, delays and unexpected changes to your route.

Ignoring the camping equipment list

Confirm what the vehicle rental includes before the trip so you do not discover missing equipment later.

Planning unrealistic driving days

Safari roads can be slow. Build enough time into your itinerary instead of treating every route like a highway journey.

Treating wildlife like ordinary camping

Follow campsite instructions and never assume animals are far away simply because you cannot see them.

Entering remote areas without preparation

Fuel, food, water, navigation and recovery planning become more important as you move away from towns.

Frequently asked questions

Tanzania self-drive camping FAQ

Can I camp during a Tanzania self-drive safari?

Yes. Camping is a practical option for independent travelers, provided you use authorized campsites and follow the rules of the relevant park or conservation authority.

What is the difference between a public and special campsite?

Public campsites generally provide simpler shared facilities, while special campsites can provide a more private camping experience. Requirements and availability vary by location.

Is a roof tent better than a ground tent?

Both can work well. A roof tent provides a raised sleeping platform and is designed around the vehicle, while a ground tent provides a different type of flexibility. The best choice depends on your group and vehicle.

Where can I buy groceries for a Tanzania self-drive camping safari?

Arusha is usually the best place to do the main grocery shop. Travelers can use supermarkets such as Village Supermarket and Shoppers Supermarket as well as local markets. Karatu and Mto wa Mbu can also be useful for topping up basic supplies before continuing towards Ngorongoro and Serengeti.

Can I buy groceries in Karatu?

Karatu is a useful resupply stop on the northern safari route. However, availability can vary, so it is sensible to purchase important long-lasting supplies in Arusha before starting the remote part of the trip.

Can I buy groceries in Mto wa Mbu?

Mto wa Mbu is another practical place to top up basic food supplies and fresh produce before continuing towards the Ngorongoro and Serengeti areas.

Can I camp anywhere in Tanzania?

No. Camping is regulated in national parks and protected areas. Use authorized campsites and follow the rules of the authority managing the area.

What should I carry for a camping safari?

Your equipment depends on the rental package, but common requirements include a tent, sleeping equipment, cooking equipment, water containers, food, lighting, personal first-aid supplies and appropriate clothing.

Should I buy all my food before leaving Arusha?

It is usually sensible to buy the important supplies in Arusha, particularly items that you know you will need for several days. You can then use places such as Karatu and Mto wa Mbu as possible top-up points depending on your route.
