Before choosing a route

Start with the journey, not just the parks.

A Tanzania self-drive safari is different from simply connecting destinations on a map. A route that looks short on paper can take much longer because of gravel roads, wildlife stops, park gates, road conditions, weather and the amount of time you want to spend exploring.

The best itinerary is therefore not necessarily the one that visits the largest number of parks. It is the one that gives you enough time to drive safely, explore properly and recover between long driving days.

Use the frameworks below as starting points, then adjust them around your travel dates, interests and vehicle.

Choose a region

Tanzania has more than one self-drive experience.

Your route should reflect the character of the areas you want to explore. Northern Tanzania has the most established safari circuit, while southern and western routes require more time and preparation.

01 · Northern Circuit

Northern Tanzania

The classic self-drive region, linking destinations such as Tarangire, Lake Manyara, Ngorongoro and Serengeti.

Arusha → Tarangire → Manyara / Karatu → Ngorongoro → Serengeti
02 · Southern Tanzania

Southern Tanzania

A more remote style of safari involving destinations such as Nyerere and Ruaha. Allow more time for long road sections and remote travel.

Dar es Salaam → Nyerere → Ruaha → Iringa / return
03 · Western & Remote Routes

Further West

Western Tanzania can provide a much more remote road-trip experience, but distances, services and communications require more careful preparation.

Plan around fuel → road conditions → accommodation → recovery
Time-based route ideas

How many days do you have?

These are planning frameworks rather than fixed tours. Your actual route should change according to the season, park access, road conditions, accommodation and the amount of time you want to spend driving.

5 Days

Short Northern Circuit

Five days can work for a focused Northern Tanzania trip, but it is important not to overload the itinerary.

Arusha → Tarangire → Karatu / Ngorongoro → Serengeti → return
Keep the route focused and avoid adding too many long transfers.
7 Days

Classic Northern Safari

Seven days gives you more flexibility to combine several major Northern Circuit destinations while allowing time for game drives.

Arusha → Tarangire → Manyara / Karatu → Ngorongoro → Serengeti → Serengeti → return
Consider using Karatu as a practical overnight base around Ngorongoro and Manyara.
10 Days

More Time in the Wild

Ten days allows the route to breathe. Instead of treating every destination as a single overnight stop, you can spend additional nights in important wildlife areas.

Arusha → Tarangire → Manyara → Ngorongoro → Serengeti → Serengeti → Serengeti / Northern Serengeti → return
Longer stays can reduce the pressure of moving every day.
14 Days

Extended Tanzania Road Trip

Fourteen days opens up possibilities for slower Northern Circuit travel or combining safari areas with additional destinations.

Arusha → Tarangire → Manyara → Ngorongoro → Serengeti → extended Serengeti → return or continue toward another region
Do not add distant regions simply because there are extra days. Road time still matters.
Distance planning

Kilometres do not tell the whole story.

Route distances in Tanzania should always be treated as planning estimates. Inside parks and on unpaved routes, average speed can be much lower than on paved roads.

Route Approx. Distance Planning Notes
Arusha → Tarangire ~120 km One of the easier Northern Circuit transfers; final approach includes park roads.
Arusha → Lake Manyara ~125 km Mostly straightforward road travel toward Mto wa Mbu and the park entrance.
Arusha → Ngorongoro area ~175–180 km Allow extra time for the highlands, traffic, stops and access procedures.
Arusha → Serengeti ~325–340 km* A long journey involving the Ngorongoro Conservation Area and unpaved sections.
Ngorongoro → Serengeti Varies by destination Route length depends on the Serengeti gate and camp or area selected.
Serengeti Central → Northern Serengeti Route dependent Distances can look manageable on a map but park-road driving can take considerably longer.

Before you lock it in

Eight things that can change your route.

A good Tanzania self-drive itinerary is built around practical constraints as much as attractions.

1

Travel time

Decide how many actual safari days you have after arrival, departure and vehicle collection.

2

Road conditions

Gravel, corrugation, mud and rain can dramatically change the time needed for a route.

3

Park access

Park opening arrangements, gate procedures and permitted driving areas need to fit your daily schedule.

4

Fuel

Fuel planning becomes increasingly important once you leave major towns and enter remote safari areas.

5

Vehicle

Choose a vehicle based on the actual roads, season, camping equipment and number of passengers.

6

Camping or lodging

Your overnight locations can determine whether a route is comfortable or involves unnecessary backtracking.

7

Season

Wet-season conditions can slow some roads considerably, while dry-season travel can mean dust and corrugation.

8

Driving experience

A route suitable for an experienced African overlander may not feel appropriate for a first-time safari driver.

Northern Circuit

The classic self-drive route through Northern Tanzania.

Northern Tanzania is the most straightforward place to start planning a first Tanzania self-drive safari because several major wildlife destinations can be connected within one journey.

Arusha

Start here

Collect the vehicle, check equipment, buy supplies and confirm your navigation and communication setup before leaving town.

Tarangire

Baobabs & elephants

Tarangire is relatively close to Arusha and can be used as an early safari stop before continuing toward the Rift Valley.

Manyara / Karatu

A useful transition

Mto wa Mbu and Karatu can make practical bases when connecting Lake Manyara, Ngorongoro and the Serengeti route.

Ngorongoro

Plan carefully

The wider Ngorongoro Conservation Area has its own access rules. Crater access is subject to specific controlled arrangements, so build this into your itinerary rather than treating it as an ordinary park stop.

Serengeti

Allow enough time

The Serengeti is enormous. Choosing where to stay or camp matters because the distance between areas can turn a short-looking transfer into a major driving day.

Return

Do not rush the exit

Leave enough time to return the vehicle, reach your next accommodation or connect with your departure plans without creating an unnecessarily tight final day.

Build your own route

A simple way to design a Tanzania self-drive itinerary.

Instead of starting with a list of every place you could visit, begin with your available days and work outward. This prevents a common mistake: creating an impressive-looking map that leaves too little time for the actual safari.

01. Choose your arrival and departure points.
02. Decide how many full safari days you have.
03. Pick the region that matches your interests.
04. Mark the parks or areas you definitely want to visit.
05. Estimate realistic driving time between them.
06. Add fuel and supply stops.
07. Match overnight locations to the route.
08. Add a little flexibility for road conditions.

Common questions

Tanzania self-drive route FAQ

Answers to some of the route-planning questions travellers ask before driving independently in Tanzania.

What is the best Tanzania self-drive route?

There is no single route that suits every traveller. The Northern Circuit is practical for combining Tarangire, Lake Manyara, Ngorongoro and Serengeti, while longer trips can explore southern or western Tanzania.

Can I do a 7-day Tanzania self-drive safari?

Yes. Seven days can work well for a focused Northern Tanzania itinerary. The important part is balancing park time with the driving required to move between destinations.

How many days do I need for a Tanzania self-drive safari?

Five days can work for a compact itinerary, while seven, ten or fourteen days allow progressively more time for wildlife areas, longer stays and less rushed travel.

Can I drive from Arusha to Serengeti myself?

Yes. The route is possible as a self-drive journey with suitable preparation and a capable vehicle. It is a long route involving unpaved sections, the Ngorongoro Conservation Area and park procedures, so it should not be planned like an ordinary highway journey.

Can I self-drive in Southern Tanzania?

Yes, but southern routes can be more remote. Fuel, road conditions, communications, accommodation and recovery planning become particularly important.

Do I need a 4x4 for a Tanzania self-drive safari?

A suitable high-clearance 4x4 is strongly appropriate for many safari routes. Exact requirements depend on the route, season and road conditions.

Should I include every major park in one itinerary?

Not necessarily. Adding more destinations can increase driving time and reduce the amount of time available for wildlife viewing. Choose the places that fit the experience you actually want.

Are route distances in Tanzania reliable?

Distances are useful for planning, but they should not be treated as exact travel-time predictions. Road surface, weather, traffic, wildlife areas and stops can all affect the actual journey.
